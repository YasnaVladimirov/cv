## Context

The company's terms and conditions were served by a micro frontend that also
held several other, unrelated things. It was not built around one concern, and
it ran on Vue 2.

## Problem

Three problems, and they reinforced each other.

Vue 2 had reached end of life. An unmaintained framework version stops
receiving security patches, so staying on it is a standing vulnerability rather
than a piece of tech debt to schedule.

Releases were coupled. A change to the terms shipped alongside everything else
living in that application, which makes a small, low-risk edit carry the risk
of everything it travels with.

Ownership was unclear. An application covering several unrelated concerns has
no obvious owner for any one of them.

## My role

I built and released it end to end — from the project template to production.

## Approach

Upgrading the shared application in place would have solved the framework
problem and neither of the other two. Extraction solved all three at once, so I
took the terms out rather than modernising around them.

I started from the organisation's micro frontend template, moved the logic
across, wired the new application into the single-spa host, and released it as
its own deployable unit.

The reasons to extract were also the reasons to keep the result small: one
concern, one owner, one release.

## Outcome

It is live. Terms and conditions now deploy independently of the application
they came from, on a supported framework version, with a clear owner.

## Stack

Vue 3, single-spa, TypeScript.

## What I would do differently

DRAFT — approve this or replace it.

Nothing about the build was difficult, and that is worth saying plainly: the
template did its job, and the interesting decision had already been made before
any code was written. The question worth more of my time was the one I answered
quickly — extract, or upgrade in place. It was the right call here because
three problems collapsed into one piece of work, but I would want to be able to
show that rather than assert it.

## Kontekst

Uslovi korišćenja kompanije bili su deo mikro-frontend aplikacije koja je
sadržala i još nekoliko nepovezanih stvari. Nije bila građena oko jedne
namene, a radila je na Vue 2.

## Problem

Tri problema, koja su se međusobno pojačavala.

Vue 2 je izgubio podršku. Verzija radnog okvira koja se više ne održava ne
dobija bezbednosne zakrpe, pa ostanak na njoj nije tehnički dug koji se
planira, već stalna ranjivost.

Izdanja su bila povezana. Izmena uslova isporučivala se zajedno sa svim ostalim
što živi u toj aplikaciji, pa mala izmena niskog rizika nosi rizik svega sa čim
putuje.

Vlasništvo je bilo nejasno. Aplikacija koja pokriva nekoliko nepovezanih namena
nema očiglednog vlasnika ni za jednu od njih.

## Moja uloga

Izradila sam je i izdala od početka do kraja — od šablona projekta do produkcije.

## Pristup

Nadogradnja zajedničke aplikacije na licu mesta rešila bi problem radnog okvira
i nijedan od druga dva. Izdvajanje je rešilo sva tri odjednom, pa sam uslove
izdvojila umesto da ih modernizujem u postojećem okruženju.

Krenula sam od organizacionog šablona za mikro-frontend, prebacila logiku,
povezala novu aplikaciju sa single-spa domaćinom i izdala je kao zasebnu
jedinicu za postavljanje.

Razlozi za izdvajanje bili su ujedno i razlozi da rezultat ostane mali: jedna
namena, jedan vlasnik, jedno izdanje.

## Rezultat

U produkciji je. Uslovi korišćenja se sada postavljaju nezavisno od aplikacije
iz koje su potekli, na podržanoj verziji radnog okvira i sa jasnim vlasnikom.

## Tehnologije

Vue 3, single-spa, TypeScript.

NACRT — odobri ovo ili zameni.

Ništa u samoj izradi nije bilo teško, i to vredi reći otvoreno: šablon je

i jedan red koda. Pitanje koje je zasluživalo više vremena bilo je ono na koje
sam brzo odgovorila — izdvojiti ili nadograditi na licu mesta. Ovde je odluka
bila ispravna jer su se tri problema svela na jedan posao, ali bih volela da to
mogu i da pokažem, a ne samo da tvrdim.
